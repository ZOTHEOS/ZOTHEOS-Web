Where we are

457 nations in 15 regions and 99 present-day countries, with 1,285 checked facts. Thin regions come first: they are the work still to do. Tier A entries have the nation's own site and a fact from it; tier B entries have at least one checked fact; tier C entries are listed by name while facts are compiled.

A denominator is a planning number, not a definition of who counts. State lists reflect state recognition. Nations recognized by a state, nations that are not, and nations that identify themselves are listed here with the same dignity.

Names missing from profiles should still be visible

The new directory preserves 577 published US federal name records, while the source states 575. That discrepancy remains unresolved. Directory names are separate from the researched profiles counted below.

Canada's separate inventory lists 634 named administrative registry groups as of December 31, 2024, not that many current governments.

Canada: the First Nation Profiles search: 638 source records; snapshot 2026-10-12. Profile directory rows returned by the official wildcard search, observed on retrieval date. Not a verified count of current governments or all Canadian Indigenous peoples. Inuit, Metis and broader peoples require separate inventories. Unresolved source-count difference: The landing page reports 619 First Nations while the public wildcard search returned 638 distinct profile-number records. These are not automatically equivalent units. No cause has been established and no record is removed to fit the headline count.

Brazil: named 2022 census categories: 391 source records; snapshot 2022. Named self-declared ethnicity, people or group categories in Brazil’s 2022 census. These are census categories, not a count of governments, legal recognition decisions or a complete inventory of Indigenous identities. Countries indicates the census scope, not an exclusive homeland.

New researched pages: Southern Ute Indian Tribe, Ute Mountain Ute Tribe, Ute Indian Tribe, Fort Sill Apache Tribe, Tonto Apache Tribe, Lipan Apache Tribe, Chiricahua Apache Nation. Distinct communities and governments keep separate records.

Website privacy

On this device: these pages save your theme and motion choice in browser storage, and the nations page keeps a random key and its place in the walk there, so you meet new nations. None of it leaves your device.

Connections: every request these pages make goes to zotheos.org. Sources and nations' own sites open only when you follow a link. A correction is written by your own mail client to contact@zotheos.org.

Roots and gratitude

ZOTHEOS draws its visual language from ancient Mesoamerica: the stepped fret, the fine black line of the painted codices, the count of days. It honors the Indigenous peoples of the Americas: the Nahua, Maya, Zapotec and many other peoples of Mexico and Central America, and the Native nations further north. They are distinct peoples with connected roots that are still living, and many Mexican and Mexican American families carry those roots too. As the project grows, it intends to work with Indigenous scholars and community members, so that every symbol here is used accurately and with permission. Any wider work with the traditions of other peoples is meant to follow the same rule: their guidance, their permission. That work has a first page: Indigenous nations today, their own names first, every fact with its source and the date it was checked.
