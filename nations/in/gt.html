Guatemala

10 nations list Guatemala among their present-day countries.

Present-day countries are listed by ISO 3166 names. Many nations live across more than one.

10 nations

  1. Ch'orti'

    Also known as Maya Ch'orti'

    Lands Jocotán, Camotán, Olopa and Quezaltepeque in Chiquimula and La Unión in Zacapa, Guatemala, and communities around Copán in Honduras

    Ch'orti' is also spoken in Honduran communities such as Cabañas, Santa Rita and Copán Ruinas.

    Source: Agencia Guatemalteca de Noticias (AGN) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  2. Garifuna

    Also known as Garínagu

    Lands Caribbean coast of Honduras, Belize, Guatemala and Nicaragua, plus large diaspora communities

    In 2024, Belize observed Garifuna Settlement Day on 19 November as one of its official public holidays.

    Source: Government of Belize Press Office (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  3. Ixil

    Also known as Maya Ixil

    Lands The Ixil region of northern Quiché, Guatemala: Nebaj, Chajul and Cotzal

    Ixil women weavers of the Q'imb'al initiative, with youth and village ancestral authorities, launched Community Radio Tiichajil Tenam in Nebaj to share Ixil cultural values.

    Source: Cultural Survival (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  4. Kaqchikel

    Also known as Maya Kaqchikel

    Lands Central highlands of Guatemala, including Chimaltenango, Sacatepéquez and parts of Sololá

    In 2022 the United Nations Special Rapporteur on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples was a member of the Maya Kaqchikel people.

    Source: Cultural Survival (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  5. K'iche'

    Also known as Maya K'iche'

    Lands Western highlands of Guatemala, including Quiché, Totonicapán, Quetzaltenango and Sololá

    In Totonicapán, the 48 Cantones is a K'iche' ancestral authority built from community governments, with representatives chosen each year in assembly.

    Source: Cultural Survival (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  6. Mam

    Also known as Maya Mam

    Lands Western highlands of Guatemala (Huehuetenango, San Marcos, Quetzaltenango) and neighboring Chiapas, Mexico

    In October 2025, the Mam communities of Cajolá and Todos Santos Cuchumatán joined two other Maya communities in reporting to a UN committee on their community radio rights.

    Source: Cultural Survival (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  7. Mopan

    Also known as Mopan Maya

    Lands The Toledo District of southern Belize and nearby Petén, Guatemala

    In 2022 a Free, Prior and Informed Consultation Protocol for the Maya of southern Belize was adopted, and in 2025 a working draft on protecting Maya customary land tenure followed.

    Source: Caribbean Court of Justice (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  8. Q'eqchi'

    Also known as Maya Q'eqchi'

    Lands Alta Verapaz, Izabal and Petén in Guatemala, and the Toledo District of southern Belize

    Radio Chiquixji in Alta Verapaz, Guatemala, broadcasts for bilingual Q'eqchi' and Spanish speakers and for communities that speak only Q'eqchi'.

    Source: Cultural Survival (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  9. Tz'utujil

    Also known as Maya Tz'utujil

    Lands The southern shore of Lake Atitlán in Sololá, Guatemala, including Santiago Atitlán and San Pedro La Laguna

    Radio Sembrador, a community station in San Pedro La Laguna on Lake Atitlán, broadcasts news and cultural programs in Tz'utujil and Spanish.

    Source: Cultural Survival (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  10. Xinka

    Also known as Xinca

    Lands Santa Rosa, Jutiapa and Jalapa departments in southeastern Guatemala

    In May 2024 the Parliament of the Xinka People hosted Guatemala's president in San Juan de Arana, Cuilapa, where they signed a 13-point work agenda.

    Source: Agencia Guatemalteca de Noticias (AGN) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

Website privacy

On this device: these pages save your theme and motion choice in browser storage, and the nations page keeps a random key and its place in the walk there, so you meet new nations. None of it leaves your device.

Connections: Nations pages load their resources from zotheos.org. Sources and nations' own sites open when you follow a link. The separate Theater automatically loads publisher thumbnails and the selected ready player on opening the page. Their providers receive your IP address and request information under their own privacy policies. Eligible previews may start muted when in view; other stories wait for Play. A correction is written by your own mail client to contact@zotheos.org.

Roots and gratitude

ZOTHEOS draws its visual language from ancient Mesoamerica: the stepped fret, the fine black line of the painted codices, the count of days. It honors the Indigenous peoples of the Americas: the Nahua, Maya, Zapotec and many other peoples of Mexico and Central America, and the Native nations further north. They are distinct peoples with connected roots that are still living, and many Mexican and Mexican American families carry those roots too. As the project grows, it intends to work with Indigenous scholars and community members, so that every symbol here is used accurately and with permission. Any wider work with the traditions of other peoples is meant to follow the same rule: their guidance, their permission. That work has a first page: Indigenous nations today, their own names first, every fact with its source and the date it was checked.
