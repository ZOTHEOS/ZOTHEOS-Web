The Arctic, northern Europe and Russia

20 nations of The Arctic, northern Europe and Russia, each with its own name first and checked facts with sources.

20 nations

  1. Dolgan

    Also known as Долганы

    Lands Taimyr Peninsula in Krasnoyarsk Krai (Taimyr Dolgan-Nenets District) and the Anabar District and city of Yakutsk in Sakha (Yakutia), Russia

    During an August 2026 folk culture expedition to Khatanga, the Dolgan dried-fish dish kerdiileek was added to the list of Taimyr's intangible cultural heritage.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  2. Even

    Also known as Эвены

    Lands Northeastern Siberia: Sakha (Yakutia), Magadan Oblast, Kamchatka, Chukotka and Khabarovsk Krai, Russia

    The 2002 census counted 11,657 Even living in the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia).

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  3. Evenki

    Also known as Эвенки

    Lands Siberian taiga from the Ob River to the Sea of Okhotsk, with the largest group in Sakha (Yakutia), also Krasnoyarsk and Khabarovsk krais, Buryatia, Amur, Irkutsk and Zabaykalsky regions of Russia, and in China and Mongolia

    Evenki communities live across Russia, with the largest in Sakha (Yakutia), and Evenki also live in China and Mongolia.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  4. Inughuit

    Also known as Polar Inuit

    Lands Avanersuaq (the Thule region) in northwest Greenland, centred on the town of Qaanaaq

    Hunting is still a significant part of the livelihood of several families in Qaanaaq, Greenland's youngest and northernmost town.

    Source: Lex, Danmarks Nationalleksikon (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  5. Itelmen

    Also known as Ительмены

    Lands Tigilsky District on the west coast of Kamchatka Krai, with smaller groups in Chukotka and Magadan Oblast, Russia

    The self-name Itelmen means an inhabitant of dry land, a human being.

    Source: The Red Book of the Peoples of the Russian Empire (Institute of the Estonian Language archive) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  6. Kalaallit

    Also known as Greenlandic Inuit

    Lands Kalaallit Nunaat (Greenland), which has self-government within the Kingdom of Denmark

    Under the Self-Government Act, in force since 21 June 2009, the people of Greenland are recognised as a people with the right to self-determination under international law.

    Source: Prime Minister's Office of Denmark (Statsministeriet) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  7. Ket

    Also known as Кеты

    Lands Yenisei River basin, mainly Turukhansky District of Krasnoyarsk Krai, Russia

    A Cyrillic writing system that Ket speakers actually use came into being in the late 1980s.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  8. Khanty

    Also known as Ханты

    Lands River valleys of the Ob basin in western Siberia: Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug (Yugra), Yamal-Nenets Autonomous Okrug and Tomsk Oblast, Russia

    For many Khanty families, fishing and reindeer herding are closely intertwined, and family fishing hamlets on rivers like the Synia still carry clan surnames.

    Source: Arctic Anthropology blog, Arctic Centre, University of Lapland (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  9. Koryak

    Also known as Коряки

    Lands Mainly the north of Kamchatka Krai, Russian Far East

    In 2000, a Russian government decree gave the Koryak the status of an Indigenous small-numbered people.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  10. Luoravetlan

    Also known as Chukchi

    Lands Chukotka Autonomous Okrug, northern Kamchatka Krai and the Nizhnekolymsky District of Sakha (Yakutia), Russia

    Researchers at Russia's Kunstkamera museum published a 2018 study on the mobile dwellings of Chukchi reindeer herders, covering both traditions and innovations.

    Source: Peter the Great Museum of Anthropology and Ethnography (Kunstkamera), Russian Academy of Sciences (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  11. Mansi

    Also known as Манси

    Lands Ob and Irtysh basin in western Siberia, mainly Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug (Yugra), with small groups in Yamal-Nenets Autonomous Okrug and Sverdlovsk Oblast, Russia

    Mansi rapper Yevgeny Anyamov, known as Bizzo, records his songs in Mansi and opened his own studio in his hometown of Ivdel to share his people's culture with youth.

    Source: Fenno-Ugria Foundation (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  12. Nanai

    Also known as Нанайцы

    Lands Lower Amur River valley in Khabarovsk Krai and Primorsky Krai, Russia, and the Sungari River area of northeast China

    Nanai communities also live in China, where about 4,600 Nanai were counted in 2000.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  13. Nenets

    Also known as Ненцы

    Lands Tundra and forest-tundra from the Kola Peninsula to the Yenisei River, including the Nenets and Yamal-Nenets autonomous okrugs and Taimyr, Russia

    In Yamal, a regional grant helps young Nenets, Khanty and other Indigenous nomadic families with three or more children equip a chum shelter. By 2024, 268 families had received it.

    Source: Fenno-Ugria Foundation (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  14. Nganasan

    Also known as Нганасаны

    Lands Southern and central Taimyr Peninsula, Krasnoyarsk Krai, including the villages of Ust-Avam, Volochanka and Novaya and the towns of Dudinka and Norilsk, Russia

    In August 2026, a state folk culture expedition in Taimyr met a knowledge keeper of Vadeyev Nganasan culture, who showed traditional dishes and how Nganasan cuisine differs from Dolgan.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  15. Nivkh

    Also known as Нивхи

    Lands Lower Amur River in Khabarovsk Krai and Sakhalin Island, Russia

    Nivkh life was built on fishing and hunting, including sea mammals taken with spears or with nets woven from leather straps.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  16. Odul, Wadul

    Also known as Yukaghir

    Lands Kolyma River basin in the Republic of Sakha (Yakutia) and Magadan Oblast, Russia

    A Cyrillic-based writing system for Yukaghir was developed in the 1970s.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  17. Sámi

    Also known as Saami

    Lands Sápmi, across northern Norway, Sweden and Finland and the Kola Peninsula of Russia

    About 10,000 Sámi live in Finland, where three Sámi languages are spoken: Northern Sámi, Inari Sámi and Skolt Sámi.

    Source: Sámediggi (Sámi Parliament of Finland) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  18. Selkup

    Also known as Селькупы

    Lands Between the middle Ob and Yenisei rivers: the Taz River area of Yamal-Nenets Autonomous Okrug and the Narym area of Tomsk Oblast, Russia

    Since a 2000 government decree, Russia has listed the Selkup as an Indigenous small-numbered people.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  19. Udehe

    Also known as Udege

    Lands Villages on the Khor and Bikin rivers in Khabarovsk Krai and Primorsky Krai, and towns of both regions, Russia

    Most Udehe today live in villages on the Khor and Bikin rivers in Russia's Far East.

    Source: RAIPON (Association of Indigenous Small-Numbered Peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East) (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

    Open this nation

  20. Yupik

    Also known as Yupigyt

    Lands Settlements on the eastern coast of the Chukchi Peninsula, Chukotka Autonomous Okrug, Russia, across the Bering Strait from Alaska

    Inuit from Chukotka became part of the Inuit Circumpolar Council in 1992, a goal that was in ICC's vision from the very beginning.

    Source: Inuit Circumpolar Council Canada (opens in a new tab) · Checked 8 Oct 2026

Website privacy

On this device: these pages save your theme and motion choice in browser storage, and the nations page keeps a random key and its place in the walk there, so you meet new nations. None of it leaves your device.

Connections: Nations pages load their resources from zotheos.org. Sources and nations' own sites open when you follow a link. The separate Theater automatically loads publisher thumbnails and the selected ready player on opening the page. Their providers receive your IP address and request information under their own privacy policies; playback waits for Play. A correction is written by your own mail client to contact@zotheos.org.

Roots and gratitude

ZOTHEOS draws its visual language from ancient Mesoamerica: the stepped fret, the fine black line of the painted codices, the count of days. It honors the Indigenous peoples of the Americas: the Nahua, Maya, Zapotec and many other peoples of Mexico and Central America, and the Native nations further north. They are distinct peoples with connected roots that are still living, and many Mexican and Mexican American families carry those roots too. As the project grows, it intends to work with Indigenous scholars and community members, so that every symbol here is used accurately and with permission. Any wider work with the traditions of other peoples is meant to follow the same rule: their guidance, their permission. That work has a first page: Indigenous nations today, their own names first, every fact with its source and the date it was checked.
