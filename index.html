Architecture

Three minds. One grounded answer.

Like a medical conference, three specialists examine the question from different angles, then deliver one careful synthesis. Analyst checks the facts. Humanist reads the room. Skeptic asks what's missing.

Fusion architecture

Three local GGUF models, Analyst, Humanist, Skeptic, each examine your query from a different angle. Their perspectives are scored by core weights derived from query type, cross-probed against each other for weak claims, then synthesized into one answer with a parsed self-reported confidence tag for every response.

Local-first

Runs on your hardware via llama.cpp. No cloud dependency. Your conversations, vault, and persistent memory never leave your device.

Evidence vault

Local knowledge base with tiered sourcing. Cross-encoder reranking. Citations enforced as [Vault:Source] with strict-mode checking.

Anti-consensus Skeptic

The Skeptic argues against the strongest emerging consensus between the other two cores, not against the user. Catches the place where smart panelists agree without checking each other.

Calibration measured

Every response carries three confidence signals, heuristic (evidence-derived), model-stated (parsed from the synthesizer), and blended. The system is honest about how confident it has the right to be.

Frequently asked

Questions worth asking.

Does ZOTHEOS actually work offline?
Yes, completely. Inference runs locally via llama.cpp. Once the models are on disk, your machine can be air-gapped and ZOTHEOS still reasons across all three cores, queries the local vault, and synthesizes a fused answer.
Why three models instead of one larger one?
A single model averages over its own biases and presents the result as a single voice. Three cores with distinct roles, Analyst, Humanist, and Skeptic, reason independently. The synthesizer then attributes claims and surfaces disagreement, so you see the structure of the answer, not just its surface.
What hardware do I need?
Almost any machine. ZOTHEOS measures your hardware and seats the council it can honestly run: a full council on a 16GB+ VRAM workstation, a mid council on a 6GB+ VRAM laptop, a light council on an 8GB CPU-only laptop, and a single small voice on pocket-class devices, honestly labeled as one mind rather than a council.
Is any of my data collected?
No accounts, no telemetry, no usage analytics. The only point of contact is the waitlist form, which collects an email solely for launch notifications. Same privacy posture as the product itself.
How is ZOTHEOS funded?
Independently. No venture capital, no investors, no advertising. Development is self-funded by the founder with optional supporter donations via PayPal, Stripe, or Gumroad. The architecture choice is the funding choice; there is no investor pressure pushing the product toward surveillance.
What is the "evidence vault"?
A local, tiered knowledge base the three cores can cite. Sources are reranked by a cross-encoder and citations are enforced in the response as [Vault:Source] tags. Strict mode rejects answers that cannot be grounded.
How do I know it is really offline?
You can watch it prove itself. ZOTHEOS keeps a network ledger and includes a canary that attempts an outbound call in front of you so you can watch it get refused. Signed result receipts can also be verified with no connection at all.
What happens when ZOTHEOS is wrong?
Corrections are first-class. Every user-flagged correction is persisted with provenance in an immutable log and approved or rejected in the open. When the council changes its mind, the full belief-drift chain is kept with the evidence that moved it. Nothing is memory-holed.
Can kids use ZOTHEOS?
Yes. Kids Mode ships with age bands, and the same privacy posture applies: everything stays on the device.
What is the Smart Board?
An infinite offline AI whiteboard. Ask the council on the canvas, drop in a document, chart data, use your voice, and save the board as a conversation.
